---
title: Inviting a teacher to join your center
summary: The email invite, the fallback link when mail lands in spam, and why you never set their password.
---

You **never set a password** for a teacher. You send an invite, and they choose their own. That isn't extra friction — it's what makes the password theirs alone from the very first moment.

## Sending the invite

1. Open **Teachers** in the dashboard.
2. Click **Invite teacher**.
3. Enter their email address.
4. Send.

<Shot caption="The invite-teacher dialog" />

The account is created immediately in a pending state, and the teacher receives an email with a one-time link to set their own password.

## If the email doesn't arrive

It happens — mail lands in spam. That's why the same screen hands you a **copyable link** you can deliver any other way (WhatsApp, or literally in person).

The link is **single-use** and expires. If it has expired, just send a fresh invite.

## Re-inviting an already-active account?

Refused, deliberately. If the account is active and has a password, a second invite would mean a center owner could reset a teacher's password — a door we do not open. A teacher who forgot their password uses **Forgot password** on the sign-in page.

## A teacher who already has an Edura account

If the teacher is already on Edura, the path isn't an invite — it's a **collaboration request**. You send them the subject, grade, hall and schedule, and they accept or decline. Acceptance is what creates the class and its timetable.

> The short version: an invite creates a new account, a collaboration request links an existing one.

Once the teacher is in, you can [create your first group](/help/create-group).
