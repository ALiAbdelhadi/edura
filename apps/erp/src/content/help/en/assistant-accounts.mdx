---
title: Assistant accounts and the limits on them
summary: An assistant can do everything the teacher does except money — and every action is recorded as "on behalf of".
---

A teacher with many groups can't hold all of it alone. An assistant takes the operational load without ever touching the money.

## Inviting an assistant

From the teacher dashboard → **Team** → **Invite assistant**. You enter their email and they set their own password — you never set one for them, exactly as with [inviting a teacher](/help/invite-teacher).

<Shot caption="The teacher's team screen and the invite dialog" />

## One assistant, one teacher

An assistant works with **exactly one teacher**. That isn't a tidiness rule — it is enforced in the database, so "on behalf of whom" can never be ambiguous.

## What an assistant can do

Everything in the day-to-day work:

- Attendance, including the hall-door check-in
- Grades and teaching materials
- Schedules and timetables
- Collaboration requests with centers
- The attendance-mode setting
- Messaging

## What an assistant cannot do

- **The wallet** — they can't see it and can't request a payout from it
- **Payouts and financial settings**
- **Managing other assistants**

These limits are not a hidden menu item. The request itself is refused by the server if anyone tries to cross them.

## Everything is recorded

Anything an assistant does is written to the audit trail under **their own name**, marked as **on behalf of** the teacher. You'll never look at an action and not know who really did it.

> An assistant messages people **as themselves**, not as the teacher. An adult must never speak to a minor behind someone else's identity — that rule does not bend.

## Removing an assistant

When you remove an assistant their access stops **immediately**, and any outstanding invite to them is void. You don't have to go hunting for the link you sent.
