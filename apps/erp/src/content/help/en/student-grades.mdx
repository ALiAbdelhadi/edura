From **Grades** in the menu. Everything your teacher has recorded for you is here.

<Shot src="/help/student-grades-en.webp" caption="The grades screen — summary and your subjects" />

## The summary at the top

- **Latest grade** — the most recent assessment marked for you: the percentage, the band, and the assessment's name and date. It's the first thing you look for after an exam.
- **Best subject** — the highest percentage you've reached, and which subject it's in

## Your subjects

Each subject is one row:

- **the subject's name**
- **how many grades** are recorded in it — that's what the percentage was worked out from
- **the percentage**, with **your marks over the totals** beneath it
- **the band**

### Seeing each assessment on its own

Tap any subject to open it and see **the assessments behind it one by one**: the assessment's name, its date, your score out of the total, and the percentage.

That's what answers "what did I get in the first unit exam?" — an overall percentage never says that.

## The bands

- **Excellent** — 85% and above
- **Very good** — 75% – 84%
- **Good** — 65% – 74%
- **Pass** — 50% – 64%
- **Weak** — Below 50%

**The pass mark is 50%.** Below that counts as weak.

Each band has one fixed colour across the whole platform, so you can read your level at a glance without reading the number.

## Your overall average

The figure labelled **Average Grade** on [Home](/help/student-home-and-schedule) is the average across all your grades. Under it on the same screen is a quick **per-subject** view, which links here.

## Things you might run into

**"No grades"** — your teacher hasn't recorded any yet. Recording is something they do; it isn't automatic.

**A subject isn't in the table** — it has no grades yet, even if you're subscribed to it.

**A grade is wrong** — talk to your teacher. They're the one who can change it; you can't edit your own grades.

**The percentage isn't what you expected** — a subject's percentage is **your marks summed, divided by the totals summed** — the way a school report is worked out, not the mean of each assessment's own percentage. A 100-mark exam therefore weighs more than a 10-mark quiz. Tap the subject to see the entries it came from.
