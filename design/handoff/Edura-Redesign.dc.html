The direction

Two scripts, one book.

Classical gave you hairlines, stroke instead of fill, and elevation as a whisper — the same austerity the Ledger already had. That part stays. What it could not give you was Arabic: Cormorant and Lora ship no Arabic glyphs, so your default locale was rendering in a silent system fallback.

So Arabic is not adapted from the Latin here — it is set beside it. Amiri for Arabic display, Cormorant Garamond for Latin. Both are revivals of historical printing types, which is why they sit together instead of one deferring to the other.

The gold is demoted to a stroke — rules, marks, the underline on a live link. It never fills a button. Every figure stays IBM Plex Mono and tabular, because that invariant was never about taste: it separates what the system computed from what a person typed.
